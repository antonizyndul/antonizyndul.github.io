Constructed action (CA) is one of those terms in sign language linguistics that everyone uses and nobody quite agrees on. This is a working note on where I currently stand.

## The problem with the term

The concept has gone through several names: *role shift*, *direct reported speech*, *character viewpoint*, *depicted action*. Each label foregrounds a different aspect — the shift in perspective, the quotative function, the iconic mapping from event to body. None of them is wrong, but none of them is complete either.

What I find most useful is thinking about CA as a **point on a continuum** rather than a discrete category. At one end you have fully lexicalised signs with no trace of enactment. At the other end you have full pantomime, where the signer's body becomes the referent completely. CA sits somewhere in the middle — and exactly where is what makes it interesting.

## Why kinematics matter

The kinematic approach, following [Jantunen et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/example), treats CA as measurable. You can ask: how much does the torso rotate? How far does the gaze shift? Does the handshape maintain lexical form or does it adapt to the depicted action?

This gives you something empirical to work with, rather than relying solely on native signer intuitions or transcription conventions.

## Where I'm heading

For my PhD, I'm treating the CA–pantomime boundary as the main object of study in PJM. The working hypothesis is that there are gradient kinematic features that covary with the degree of iconicity, and that these differ across signers and contexts in systematic ways.

More on the methodology in a future post.
